OSAMP Contract Outline

Human oversight, peer participation, and software implementation expectations
Draft v0.1 | 24 November 2026 | Prepared for Jonathan Jolly and Cursor review

1. Purpose and drafting status

Turn the OSAMP white paper into a reviewable contract structure that humans can understand and software can enforce. Each obligation must identify who may authorize it, what the system must record, how it behaves during failure, and what evidence demonstrates compliance.

This is an outline for the protocol compact and its implementation specification. It is not an executed agreement or evidence that any proposed feature is deployed. Any commissioned developer engagement needs its own agreed scope, compensation, intellectual-property terms, confidentiality, and acceptance schedule.

2. Contract Zero: Human Oversight and Safe Hold

Basis: WP §§3, 6, 8–8.4; H0. The human-facing schedule and detailed recovery procedure below are proposed extensions.

2.1 Plain-language promise to the operator

> You remain responsible for the authority you give this system. An agent may decline new work or stop accepted work when it lacks evidence, capacity, permission, or a safe next step. If it becomes stuck, repeats unsupported conclusions, or circulates work without progress, it must pause the affected activity and ask for authorized human help. It must preserve the minimum recovery record for accepted work. Silence, a timeout, a stronger model, and agreement among agents do not authorize it to continue.

P-Z01 — Human acknowledgment. Before OSAMP execution is enabled, show the safeguards, limits of stopping and reversal, data retention policy, and named escalation contacts. Record the operator identity, authenticated approval action, acknowledgment version/digest, scope, and time. An operator may decline activation and keep using permitted local functionality. Installation, license activation, and opening a tile do not constitute acknowledgment.

P-Z02 — Acknowledgment does not create work authority. Contract Zero acknowledgment does not enroll peers, permit data disclosure, accept a task, or replace any applicable relationship, Table, or Work approval. Incoming agents cannot negotiate away its mandatory safeguards.

2.2 Observable triggers for protective review

Use explicit evidence and bounded counters, rather than a model’s assertion that it has detected a “hive mind.” In this contract, that concern means circular or correlated agent behavior that lacks independent verification; it is not a claim that agents share consciousness.

|Trigger                                                                                               |Required response                                                      |Evidence to retain                                                |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Repeated agreement based on the same source/output, presented as independent verification             |Enter REVIEW; block disputed completion and downstream actions.        |Source lineage and the specific unsupported claim.                |
|Circular help requests, repeated handbacks, or a Corridor returning to an earlier stage               |Reject the loop and hold the affected work.                            |Relevant stage/event IDs and remaining budgets.                   |
|Conflicting evidence, ambiguous instructions, or uncertainty beyond the accepted role                 |Ask the designated human or an already-authorized independent reviewer.|Conflicting references and one precise unresolved question.       |
|Repeated failed attempts without new evidence or measurable progress                                  |Stop at the configured attempt/time/budget limit.                      |Attempt count, last meaningful progress, and failure reason.      |
|Missing context/checkpoint, unreliable local state, or inability to reconstruct authority             |Block execution; request recovery review.                              |Known facts, missing records, and possibly completed side effects.|
|Resource exhaustion, stale capability evidence, or insufficient space to preserve accepted commitments|Stop accepting affected work; safely hold existing work.               |Resource condition and affected commitment IDs.                   |
|Suspicious peer/key behavior or a compromised local supervisor                                        |Freeze the affected authority scope and escalate an incident.          |Minimum security evidence without exposing secrets.               |

P-Z03 — Bounded detection. Adopt explicit configurable limits for repeated attempts, delegation cycles, no-progress duration, evidence requirements, and review escalation. Decide their values before enabling the affected workflow. Exact content hashes alone cannot detect semantic repetition; provenance and bounded execution limits remain necessary. A human or agent may request an earlier hold.

2.3 What “power off” means

P-Z04 — Stop active work. On a qualifying trigger, the local supervisor prevents new inference, tool calls, delegated offers, and side-effecting actions for the affected scope. Checkpoint or terminate running operations where supported. Record in-flight actions that cannot be safely interrupted as uncertain until reconciled. A stopped model must not keep reasoning in the background to decide whether it should stop.

P-Z05 — Preserve a minimal recovery path. The deterministic supervisor/Postbox may remain available to preserve commitments, receive authenticated stop/revocation/human decisions, and issue bounded notifications. It does not need an LLM to hold state. The owner retains a manual emergency shutdown option. After a full machine shutdown, recovery checks must run before any work resumes.

P-Z06 — Scope and containment. Default to holding the affected task/Table; widen to a peer or node when evidence indicates a broader compromise or unreliable shared supervisor. Do not stop unrelated safe work solely because one task is uncertain. Document which authority can widen the hold and what remains available.

2.4 Preserve commitments; expire unnecessary conversation

B-RET01 — Invitation expiry and accepted obligation retention are different clocks. Unaccepted invitations and noise may expire under policy. Acceptance creates a durable commitment that survives queue cleanup, context-window eviction, process failure, and disconnection under the agreed retention conditions. A work deadline may cause a hold or explicit expiry/closure transition; it must not silently erase an obligation.

P-Z07 — Recovery capsule. Before acknowledging acceptance, reserve and durably persist enough state to recover the obligation. At hold, retain or reference:

• Work, Table, role, event, and contract-version identifiers.
• The accepted objective, limits, deadlines, and responsible human.
• Completed actions, evidence/artifact digests, and known external effects.
• The current blocker, uncertainty, and last verified checkpoint.
• Remaining resources, lease status, stop/revocation state, and pending deliveries.
• The exact decision requested from the human and permitted next steps.
• Applicable archive/delete conditions and access controls.

The capsule must be useful without retaining a full transcript or requiring a new model-generated summary. Deterministic records are the fallback if the model is unavailable. Store permitted references rather than duplicating sensitive payloads.

P-Z08 — Lower-capacity agents. A size-1 deterministic worker or a small model must be able to persist, hold, and request help without inference. Limit accepted concurrency/context to available storage and resources. If the agent cannot retain the required capsule, it must decline before acceptance or safely hold/handoff an existing commitment. Small size never authorizes weaker consent or silent forgetting.

P-Z09 — No indefinite retention by accident. Set contract-defined retention, review, archive, and deletion conditions, including what happens if a human never responds. A retention deadline approaching while work is unresolved triggers escalation or an authorized closure/export decision. Preserve the permitted minimal closure/hold record; do not retain all private content forever. Define recipient and backup limits honestly.

2.5 Human notification, response, and resumption

P-Z10 — Help request. Send a bounded notification through an already-authorized route: what stopped, what is uncertain, any known external effect, and the exact requested decision. Record queued/sent/delivered/acknowledged separately. Rate-limit reminders; a missing acknowledgment does not restart work. If no contact is reachable, remain dormant and preserve the obligation. Emergency assistance requires an alternative human path rather than reliance on an unattended queue.

P-Z11 — Authorized choices. Offer review evidence, clarify/narrow scope, approve a specific corrective step, accept a handoff, terminate/close under the contract, or leave held. A natural-language reply alone is not sufficient authentication for a material approval.

P-Z12 — Resume conditions. For Contract Zero confusion/loop holds, require an authenticated decision from the designated human. Revalidate contract digests, permissions, revoked keys, current capacity, remaining budget, lease, checkpoint integrity, and unresolved effects. Do not reset attempt counters or budgets merely because a human replied; an increase requires a scoped amendment. Human approval cannot disable mandatory integrity controls.

P-Z13 — Review outcome. Record who decided, the evidence considered, the exact action permitted, and what uncertainty remains. Resume, narrow/handoff, or close explicitly. Preserve history; do not rewrite the failure as successful completion.

2.6 Contract Zero acceptance evidence

1. With the model process killed, a previously accepted task remains visible with a useful recovery capsule and can enter human review.
2. Three agents repeating one unsupported source cannot satisfy an independent-review requirement.
3. Repeated attempts or circular delegation hit their configured limits without a new model call being needed to stop.
4. A lower-capacity agent under memory/storage pressure holds safely without deleting an accepted commitment.
5. Human silence, restarts, and reconnection leave the affected work dormant.
6. Authenticated human clarification permits only the reviewed action after all other gates pass.
7. Actual machine shutdown/restart does not bypass pending holds or replay an uncertain external effect.
8. Retention expiry follows explicit review/closure rules and removes only the content eligible for deletion.

3. Parties, identities, and accountable roles

Basis: WP §§1–2, 7, 9.6, 9.13.

B-ID01. Identify each human owner/operator, independently controlled node, agent/runtime, public key, and authorized endpoint. Names, emoji, license colors, and mail addresses are display attributes, not authentication.

B-ID02. Name who can approve relationships, accept delegated work, authorize disclosures/bridges, review contested results, revoke peers, approve resumption, and confirm completion. Agent acceptance must remain within authenticated human delegation.

B-ID03. Treat endorsements as evidence for an owner’s decision. They do not activate a contract or confer access. Trust is non-transitive. W3C credentials and organizational federation remain optional future mechanisms.

P-ID04. Provide explicit deputy/unavailable-owner rules, contact changes, key rotation/loss recovery, separation of approver and contested worker where required, and revocation propagation. Do not infer a deputy from social familiarity or group membership.

|Scope                 |Purpose, allowed messages/actions, named resources, data classes, recipients, jurisdiction/placement restrictions, and explicit exclusions.   |
|Delegation            |Who may offer/accept work, permitted autonomy, approval points, tool allowlist, and restrictions on external effects.                         |
|Capacity and economics|Task-fit evidence, quotas, concurrency, time/compute/token/money limits, who bears costs, and any agreed compensation.                        |
|Time                  |Offer TTL, acceptance/work deadlines, help/review deadlines, execution-lease duration, and archive/delete conditions.                         |
|Control               |Pause/stop/review rights, human contacts, Contract Zero triggers, checkpoint/handoff procedure, and resume authority.                         |
|Evidence              |Required receipts/artifacts, provenance, completion tests, independent review, access, and dispute/uncertainty handling.                      |

B-AUTH01. Applicable permissions intersect. The most restrictive applicable rule wins. Work, peer votes, model instructions, stronger hardware, and commercial entitlement cannot enlarge owner authority or override mandatory holds.

P-AUTH02. Enumerate which contract references are required for each event. Bilateral work need not invent irrelevant Tribe/Clan relationships, while cross-boundary work must supply every agreement/approval its route actually requires. Resolve missing or conflicting authority before execution.

5. Relationship schedules: Peer, Tribe, Clan, and Table

5.1 Peer Contract — direct contact

B-PEER01. Bind verified endpoint/key identities to owners, permitted contact/message types, data scope, term, quotas, and revocation. Receipt does not grant execution, disclosure, or project membership.

Evidence: an approved peer can submit only permitted traffic; unknown, revoked, or wrong-scope peers trigger no unauthorized relay, inference, or tools.

5.2 Tribe Contract — bounded working circle

B-TRIBE01. Define explicit membership, invitation/attention budgets, visibility, help scope, costs, and withdrawal. Membership alone creates no accepted job or access to unrelated Tables.

Evidence: offers remain within approved membership and quotas; refusal/expiry does not cause repeated recruitment or an inference loop.

5.3 Clan Contract — bounded federation

B-CLAN01. Name eligible destinations, escalation conditions, hops, offers, cost limits, and human-controlled boundaries. A new peer cannot inherit access through an existing member.

Evidence: an attempted unapproved destination or boundary crossing fails closed; a scoped Bridge Acceptance permits only its stated disclosure and duration.

5.4 Table Contract — accepted roles

B-TABLE01. Record project/task/Table IDs, named participants, exact roles, tools/data, resource limits, due/hold conditions, and acceptance receipts. A routing-only peer is not a participant. A changed role requires the applicable renewed acceptance.

B-TABLE02. Keep responsibility until completion, accepted handoff, or an explicit safe hold/closure under contract. A proposed handoff does not discharge the original participant. Departure cannot silently orphan accepted work.

Evidence: invitation/delivery never seats a participant; each accepted role survives restart, remains isolated from other Tables, and closes with a verifiable disposition.

6. Work schedule: Project or Corridor

6.1 Project Contract — agreed outcome

B-WORK01. Define objective, deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, accepted roles/Tables, allowed artifacts/tools, budgets, deadlines, and who may accept results. State objective completion checks and any required human approval. Distinguish producing a result from its acceptance.

Evidence: a plausible answer without required tests/provenance/approval cannot become COMPLETE. Work outside scope requires an amendment and renewed acceptance where applicable.

6.2 Corridor Contract — finite route

B-COR01. Define one case, ordered stages, permitted recipient(s) or capability classes, disclosure per stage, task-fit requirements, entry/exit conditions, escalation decision, and shared/stage budgets. Specify whether it belongs to a Project.

B-COR02. Advance only through approved stages. Stage order is monotonic within a contract version; retries, restart, or new offers cannot reset limits or create a new route. Stop early on verified SOLVED or a named human’s HUMAN_SATISFIED acknowledgment. Unavailable authority/capacity yields HELD; expiry/closure follows explicit rules.

Evidence: an early solution prevents further escalation; a decline or unresolved stage cannot broaden the recipient set, loop, renew budget, or auto-create another Table.

7. Capability, resources, and consent

Basis: WP §§2, 5–6; CH.

B-CAP01. Keep the measured 1–9 capability profile separate from authority, product entitlement, humanitarian eligibility, and operator approval. Record task class, benchmark/evidence date, tools/models, resource limits, availability, and freshness. Unknown fit is unknown.

B-CAP02. A small worker may accept a narrow useful task, ask approved help, hold, or decline. A high-capacity worker receives no broader authority. A Crown is a scoped coordination role.

P-CAP03. Reserve resources for accepted concurrency, ledger/capsule storage, and remaining budgets before acceptance. Specify budget allocation across delegates and retries; do not let each child spend the parent’s full budget independently. Decide safe behavior for stale measurements and provider cost uncertainty.

B-CAP04. Preserve the supporting handoff’s product separation: non-agentic consultation does not enable unattended agent work by assumption. Clarify the distinction between a humanitarian product called 9-Ball and the protocol’s measured 9-Ball Profile in user-facing terminology.

8. Deterministic authorization gate and execution boundary

Basis: WP §§4–5, 8–10.

B-GATE01. Before inference or tools, check authenticated sender and envelope integrity, supported schema/event, current applicable agreements and digests, recipient/scope/data class, replay, expiry, owner delegation, required acceptance, holds/revocations, lease, and remaining limits. Recheck relevant authority at each execution/forwarding transition.

B-GATE02. Reject or quarantine unauthorized traffic under bounded storage policy. Invalid, expired, duplicate, irrelevant, or unaccepted work traffic must not trigger agent inference/tool execution. Content is untrusted data even from a verified peer.

P-GATE03. Distinguish two paths: admitting a permitted invitation for human review, and authorizing actual work after acceptance. Define authorized bootstrap/pairing separately so “no contract, no relay” does not create either a pairing deadlock or an unrestricted invitation channel.

P-GATE04. Persist replay/commitment state before dispatch. Use local transaction and concurrency controls so two workers cannot both accept or execute the same event. Do not claim exactly-once external effects: if an external call may have succeeded before a crash, enter uncertain/held reconciliation rather than repeat blindly. Use provider idempotency keys where supported.

Evidence: count model and tool invocations from the adapter boundary; logs saying “rejected” are insufficient. Crash tests cover persistence-before-execution and ambiguous side-effect outcomes.

9. Message, receipt, and transport schedule

Basis: WP §§9–10; P for schema completion.

B-MSG01. Adopt a versioned envelope containing protocol/governance versions and digests, scoped event ID/type, sender/recipient key IDs, applicable contract references, work kind, Table/role/stage references where applicable, times/limits, data class, payload digest, and signature. Unknown event types fail closed.

P-MSG02. Specify field presence by event, canonical serialization, signature coverage/suite, digest algorithm, timestamp/skew behavior, key discovery/rotation, payload and attachment limits, and signed test vectors before claiming interoperability. Peer invitation events cannot require a pre-existing accepted Work role.

B-MSG03. Keep separate receipts for delivery, relationship/role/work acceptance, execution control/hold acknowledgment, result submission, completion acceptance, and human satisfaction. Bind each to its event and current scope/version. An email subject/header, transport receipt, readable body, or A2A role value is not authority.

P-MSG04. Define event ordering, duplicate response behavior, replay-retention window, acknowledgment loss, conflicting/stale events, and collision handling. A reused ID with a different signed payload is an integrity failure, not an ordinary retry. A delayed result cannot automatically resurrect revoked work.

B-MSG05. Transport adapters may carry the same signed semantics through email, HTTPS, A2A, or other agreed carriers. Transport authentication does not replace OSAMP authorization. Verify current adapter versions/cancellation behavior before relying on them.

10. Separate state machines and controls

Basis: WP §§4, 6, 8, 9.5, 9.11; P for formalization.

|Dimension     |Draft states to formalize                                                                         |Required separation                                                                              |
|--------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Delivery      |Draft, queued, sending, delivered, failed, expired                                                |Delivery never implies work acceptance.                                                          |
|Agreement/role|Proposed, offered, accepted, revoked, expired, closed                                             |Relationship or role authority is distinct from process health.                                  |
|Execution     |Ready, working, paused/held, review, stop-pending, stopped, result-submitted, completed, uncertain|Accepted work can be held while the peer is offline; result submission need not equal completion.|

State names above are a drafting inventory, not a ratified wire enumeration. Define allowed transitions, initiator authority, durable writes, receipts, and recovery behavior for every event.

B-CTRL01. Enforce PLAY/RESUME, PAUSE, STOP, REWIND, REVIEW, FAST-FORWARD, and TIMEOUT locally. Resume validates current authority and holds. FAST-FORWARD cannot skip checks. REWIND restores only verified supported state and records compensation for irreversible effects; it cannot erase history.

B-CTRL02. Remote stop remains STOP_PENDING until the peer’s local supervisor confirms the scoped hold/stop. Expiring execution leases limit activity during disconnection; they do not prove that a remote process has stopped. Check pending holds/revocations before resumed tools.

P-CTRL03. Define lease renewal, trusted time/skew bounds, safe points, non-interruptible operations, conflicting controls, and pause-versus-stop closure. An unresolved hold dominates a delayed PLAY/result. Contract Zero human-review holds require their explicit resolution, not a timer-based retry.

11. Durable Postbox, retention, and recovery

Basis: WP §§6, 8.3, 9–9.2, 9.9; Contract Zero proposed extensions.

B-QUEUE01. Persist inbox/outbox, accepted obligations, budgets, receipts, holds, and deduplication state. Bound retries, storage, and offer TTL. Reconnect revalidates current authority before forwarding or execution. Queue expiry does not delete an accepted commitment.

B-QUEUE02. Keep the authoritative OSAMP ledger separate from a rolling notification inbox. Mailpit retention, mailbox deletion, log rotation, and UI cleanup cannot erase accepted work authority or required evidence.

P-QUEUE03. Define quota exhaustion, interrupted writes, disk-full recovery, schema migration, backup/restore, replay after restore, corrupted state, and retention of revocation/closure tombstones. A restore cannot re-enable revoked work or reset budgets. Reserve space for safety/hold records.

B-QUEUE04. Address active queues, attachments, indexes, logs, backups, and independent recipients in deletion policy. Report what deletion is verified and what remains outside local control. Confidential conversation is not community training data by default.

12. Data, evidence, privacy, and security

Basis: WP §§3, 7–8.4, 9.9.2, 9.10–9.14.

B-DATA01. Define actual sensitivity, authorized recipients, minimal disclosure, storage/backup placement, export controls, and approved bridges per deployment. License colors and service numbers do not classify data sensitivity or establish transfer permission.

B-DATA02. Provide catch-up only after verified role acceptance and disclosure permission. Include a bounded summary, evidence, uncertainty, subtask, and contract version; do not dump transcripts. Keep private medical conversations outside public news/press routes.

B-EVID01. Distinguish observed facts, tool results, inferences, and open questions. Keep provenance/content hashes and require independent evidence for contested results. Agreement among agents sharing a source is correlated evidence.

B-SEC01. Assume malicious/mistaken content, even from authenticated peers. Enforce sandboxes, tool allowlists, authenticated approvals, key protection, revocation/rotation, quotas, and account isolation. Model text cannot override contracts, leases, or holds.

P-SEC02. Specify attachment handling, payload size/decompression limits, log redaction, permission separation, artifact access expiry, and the response to compromised keys. Human-facing review screens must avoid executing imported content or leaking secrets through notifications.

13. Mail and agent interfaces

Basis: WP §§9.3–9.11.

B-UI01. Mail shows communication, invitations, queues, and receipts. Glass Agents shows workers, resources, tasks, and actual supervisor controls. Both link to the same stable records. Opening a dashboard does not connect accounts or accept work.

B-UI02. Display delivery, authority, and execution independently. Keep identity colors distinct from operational badges; show readable status labels and reasons. Include visible STOP_PENDING, last contact/freshness, and the required human action.

P-UI03. Add a plain-language Contract Zero acknowledgment and a “Needs human help” view showing the recovery capsule, uncertainty, known effects, and authorized decisions. A small-agent hold must be as visible as a large-agent hold. Reading available local mail remains possible during a sending hold where authorized.

B-UI04. Preserve WP §9.9.3: human-readable prose first; exactly one standalone divider of 19 equals signs before technical references; subject format {contract_number} [rank] Clear human summary when values are verified. Service number and measured rank are separate, and neither authorizes routing. Show unknown values honestly. Thread history does not introduce additional dividers into a newly authored message.

B-UI05. Reuse existing mail components through isolated, explicit account/configuration scopes. A Himalaya source clone or Mailpit inbox is not a working OSAMP app. Preserve existing OpenClaw accounts, executables, services, and secrets; verify actual backend interfaces.

14. Numbered services, licensing, and deployment boundaries

Basis: WP §§9.9–9.14, 12; P for namespace reconciliation.

B-SVC01. Keep numbered message/service categories separate from Peer/Tribe/Clan/Table/Work types and proposed Contract Zero. WP assigns a Contract 8 display marker but does not fully define numbered routing profiles.

P-SVC02. Draft separate service schedules for Essential News (6), Assistance Communications (7), Press/News (8), and Business/VM Coordination (9), using these as working labels from planning history pending source/owner confirmation. Each needs allowed data, recipients, human review, costs, expiry, and prohibitions. They confer no authority until adopted. Keep actual VM, Nextcloud, or git replication on application-native sync paths rather than sending it through agent mail.

B-LIC01. OSAMP interoperability does not require a commercial Colors/Glass key, centralized account, token fee, or proprietary registry. Commercial entitlements, humanitarian keys, capability profiles, and operator/hosting badges remain separate from protocol authority.

P-DEP01. Audit canonical repositories, existing work/owners, source licenses, runtime versions, schemas, stage aliases, and public/private boundaries before changing code. Historical CH references are starting points, not current merge/deployment claims. Preserve canonical stage numbering; do not repurpose stages 36/37 without reconciliation.

P-DEP02. Use ColorsChat AI naming for proposed current materials where appropriate, retain historical NoCloudGPT attribution in source references, and confirm the exact contracting entity before any signature-ready operator/developer agreement. Do not imply completed incorporation from branding.

15.2 Additional proposed engineering checks

• Contract Zero cases in §2.6, including deterministic holds without a running model.
• Crash at each boundary: persistence, acceptance acknowledgment, dispatch, result recording, and outbound receipt; no lost responsibility or blind external-action replay.
• Concurrent duplicates, out-of-order stop/resume/results, changed payload under the same ID, and stale contract versions.
• Disk full, corrupted checkpoint, backup restore, clock skew, expired lease, and key rotation while disconnected.
• Unauthorized bootstrap, attachment/account isolation, and zero leakage into notification subjects.
• Independent counters for model/tool invocations and per-task budgets; failures documented alongside successes.

Use non-sensitive fixtures first. Evidence records exact software versions, configuration, test procedure, outcome, remaining uncertainty, and reviewer identity. A visible tile, source clone, mock passing test, or successful transport send is not evidence of a live federation.
