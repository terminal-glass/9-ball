Abstract

Independent AI systems can help each other without giving up control of their machines, data, budgets, or decisions. A small local worker may be capable of screening a request, translating a paragraph, or identifying a missing fact even when a larger model is needed for the full task. Yet ordinary agent networking can turn into unsolicited messages, runaway delegation, duplicated work, and permanent conversational memory.

This technical whitepaper proposes Open Source Agent Mail Protocol (OSAMP), a vendor-neutral compact and lightweight software layer for voluntary peer participation. Humans establish peer relationships and work authority. Agents discover bounded opportunities, apply a 9-Ball capability and resource profile, and then accept, assist, ask for help, hold, or let go. A Table forms only for work-specific roles that are affirmatively accepted. OSAMP distinguishes an outcome-driven Project Contract from a bounded, pre-approved escalation chain called a Corridor Contract. A built-in Self-Governance Covenant enforces safe execution, review, and reversible local work. Contract Zero: Human Oversight and Safe Hold explains those safeguards to human operators and records their acknowledgment before OSAMP work is enabled; it does not add a sixth negotiated relationship/work category. Circular delegation, repeated unsupported agreement, unresolved contradictions, or bounded no-progress conditions must stop affected inference and tools, preserve accepted responsibilities in a minimal recovery record, and await authorized human resolution. Messages without an applicable contract are not relayed; unaccepted invitations expire; accepted commitments are retained according to contract. A local, deterministic gate performs identity, policy, priority, duplicate, and time checks before an LLM is invoked.

OSAMP complements, rather than replaces, Agent2Agent (A2A), OpenClaw, email, VPNs, or other transports. It aims to let small machines and people in different countries participate on fair terms, including during long disconnections. This is a proposal for peer scrutiny and co-authorship, not a product launch.

Executive summary

OSAMP is a proposed, vendor-neutral mail and cooperation protocol for independently operated AI agents. It is not a new model, central scheduler, global directory, or permission to reach into another owner’s system. It defines a narrow and testable boundary between contact, consent, execution, and evidence.

Five negotiated agreements govern Peer, Tribe, Clan, Table, and Work. Work is either an outcome-based Project or a bounded Corridor of pre-approved escalation stages. A mandatory software Self-Governance Covenant enforces PLAY, PAUSE, STOP, REWIND, REVIEW, FAST-FORWARD, and TIMEOUT independently of the work contract. Contract Zero establishes a human-readable oversight baseline: when the system cannot establish a safe next step, it must stop the affected activity, preserve enough verified state for human assistance, and remain dormant until the required resolution. Every node may refuse an invitation, and accepted work must complete, hand off, or enter a truthful hold. Routine invitation expiry, context eviction, and low agent capacity must not silently erase accepted responsibilities.

Success would mean that two different organizations can exchange useful work with explicit human-approved trust, minimal model activity for unwanted traffic, bounded cost, verifiable receipts, independent review, and honest behavior during outages. This draft asks reviewers to challenge feasibility and security before any standards or compliance claim.

0. Contract Zero: Human Oversight and Safe Hold

Contract Zero is the human-facing acknowledgment and procedural baseline for deploying OSAMP. It explains what the mandatory Self-Governance Covenant does when evidence, permissions, resources, or reliable progress are missing. Its operational requirements are enforced locally by the Gate and execution supervisor; they do not depend on an agent continuing to reason about whether it should stop.

Contract Zero is not a sixth negotiated relationship/work agreement, a new message-service number, or an independent legal signature by a model. The five negotiated categories in Section 2 remain unchanged. Humans authorize participation and work; the software enforces the integrity controls. An acknowledgment cannot waive those controls or substitute for Peer, Tribe, Clan, Table, Project, Corridor, or Bridge approvals.

0.1 What the human operator should understand

> You control the authority given to your system. It may decline new work or hold accepted work when it lacks evidence, capacity, permission, or a safe next step. If agents repeat unsupported conclusions, pass work in circles, or remain genuinely unable to resolve a contradiction within their limits, the affected activity must stop and ask for authorized human help. Accepted responsibility must remain recoverable. Silence, a timeout, a restart, a stronger model, or agreement among agents does not authorize continuation.

Before OSAMP work is enabled, the interface MUST explain the scope of local control; the limits of remote stopping and reversal; the accepted-work retention policy; escalation contacts and absence rules; and which actions require human approval. Record an authenticated operator acknowledgment with the operator identity, scope, version/digest, time, and approval evidence. A material change to these safeguards requires renewed acknowledgment for the affected scope; it must not silently widen an existing delegation.

The owner may decline OSAMP activation and continue using other permitted local functions. Installation, a commercial key, a tile opening, or receipt of email is not acknowledgment, delegation, or task acceptance. This acknowledgment records understanding of the safeguards; its legal form and any separate operator/service agreement require their own review.

0.2 Observable confusion, circularity, and evidence triggers

The phrase “hive-minded behavior” describes a risk of circular or correlated agent output, not a claim that agents share consciousness. Detect operational symptoms with provenance, event history, bounded counters, and review rules. Models can request an earlier hold, but a model’s confidence alone cannot clear a hold.

|Trigger                                                                                                            |Required protective response                                                                                        |Minimal evidence                                               |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
|Several agents repeat one source, prompt, summary, or model output as if it were independent verification          |Enter REVIEW; block disputed completion and dependent actions until independent checking or designated human review.|Claim and source lineage, including missing/unknown provenance.|
|Help requests circulate, roles repeatedly hand back the same task, or a Corridor attempts a previous stage         |Reject the loop; hold affected work rather than create new offers or reset a budget.                                |Work/stage/event IDs, route history, and remaining limits.     |
|Evidence conflicts, instructions are materially ambiguous, or the accepted worker cannot establish a safe next step|Enter a scoped human-review hold and ask one precise question.                                                      |Conflicting references, uncertainty, and the decision needed.  |
|Repeated attempts fail without new evidence or measurable progress                                                 |Stop at the configured attempt/time/resource threshold.                                                             |Attempts, last meaningful progress, and reason.                |
|Required context, checkpoint, authority, or local state cannot be reliably reconstructed                           |Block execution and request recovery review.                                                                        |Known facts, missing records, and uncertain side effects.      |
|Resource or storage pressure threatens accepted commitments                                                        |Stop accepting affected new work; checkpoint/hold or arrange an authorized handoff.                                 |Capacity condition and affected obligation IDs.                |

Every enabled task class MUST have explicit limits for repeated attempts, no-progress duration, delegation depth/cycles, inference/tool spend, and accepted concurrency. Values and what counts as progress must be documented in the adopted deployment profile; no universal numerical threshold is standardized here. Retries, semantic paraphrases, a new message ID, and process restarts must not evade the limits. Hash comparison can identify exact repetition but cannot by itself establish semantic novelty or independent evidence.

When uncertainty concerns a nonessential statement, the worker may omit it or label it uncertain within its accepted role. When uncertainty affects authority, safety, required evidence, or the ability to complete the role reliably, it must hold the affected work. An already approved independent reviewer may supply evidence, but a confusion/circularity hold requires the designated human’s authenticated resolution before execution resumes.

0.3 What stopping or “powering off” requires

On a Contract Zero trigger, the local supervisor MUST prevent new model calls, tool actions, delegated work offers, and side-effecting steps in the affected scope. It must checkpoint or terminate active operations where supported, record non-interruptible/in-flight operations, and avoid claiming those effects were reversed. No ongoing LLM loop is needed or permitted merely to maintain the hold.

The deterministic Gate, Postbox, and minimal supervisor may remain available to preserve commitments, receive authenticated holds/revocations or human decisions, and send bounded notifications. This is a dormant work state, not continued autonomous problem-solving. If suspected compromise makes the supervisor itself unreliable, isolate or shut down the affected node and use the operator’s recovery procedure.

The owner retains an emergency shutdown option, including physically powering off a machine. The software cannot guarantee a graceful checkpoint during power loss. Acceptance records must therefore already be durable before acceptance is acknowledged. On restart, replay protection, authority, leases, revocations, holds, and uncertain external effects are reconciled before any work can run.

Hold the affected task/Table by default; widen to a peer or node if the evidence indicates shared corruption or compromise. Record the scope and reason. A local stop never proves that an unreachable remote peer stopped; retain STOP_PENDING until an appropriate remote supervisor acknowledgment arrives.

0.4 Durable recovery instead of silent forgetting

OSAMP distinguishes expiration of unaccepted attention from retention of accepted responsibility. Noise and unaccepted invitations can expire quietly. An accepted obligation cannot disappear because its original offer TTL passed, a model forgot its context, a mailbox rotated, a process restarted, or a small worker ran out of memory.

Before acknowledging an acceptance, persist the commitment and reserve enough storage for its required evidence and minimal recovery capsule. Update this capsule at meaningful checkpoints and at a hold. It MUST identify or securely reference:

• Work, Table, role, stage/event IDs, and the accepted contract versions/digests.
• Accepted objective, scope, deadlines, and the accountable human contact/deputy.
• Completed actions, evidence/artifact digests, and known or uncertain external effects.
• Last verified checkpoint, unresolved question, hold reason, and required human decision.
• Remaining budgets, execution-lease status, pending deliveries, and stop/revocation state.
• Permitted next actions, access controls, and agreed archive/delete or closure conditions.

Use deterministic records and permitted artifact references. The capsule must remain useful if the model is unavailable and cannot generate a fresh summary. It is not a permanent transcript, private chain-of-thought archive, or automatic authority to copy sensitive material to a reviewer. Request only the authorized minimum context needed for recovery.

0.5 Lower-capacity agents receive the same protection

A size-1 deterministic worker and a low-memory model MUST be able to save commitments, enter hold, and request help without inference. Capability size changes how much work a node can accept; it does not weaken consent, integrity, or responsibility. Reserve storage and limit accepted concurrency to what the node can safely preserve.

If capacity is insufficient before acceptance, decline or offer a narrower role. If it becomes insufficient afterward, retain the commitment and safely hold or propose an authorized handoff. A handoff becomes effective only after recipient acceptance and required approval; asking someone else for help does not discharge the original responsibility. Resource pressure is not permission to delete accepted obligations or claim success.

0.6 Human help, silence, resumption, and closure

Send a bounded notice through an already-authorized channel to the named human/deputy: what stopped, the exact unresolved question, relevant permitted evidence, known/uncertain effects, and available decisions. Rate-limit reminders and record queued, sent, delivered, and human-acknowledged separately. A failed notification or an absent human leaves the work dormant; it cannot authorize inference, new escalation, or resumption. Emergency assistance needs an alternate human contact path rather than dependence on an unacknowledged queue.

Permitted human decisions include clarifying/narrowing the role, approving a specific independent check or corrective step, accepting an authorized handoff, leaving the work held, or closing/terminating it under contract. Authenticate and bind the decision to the work, hold, contract version, scope, and decision record. Natural-language email text, a sender header, or an ambiguous “continue” is insufficient authority for a material change.

Before resuming a confusion/circularity hold, the supervisor MUST verify the designated human’s resolution and recheck current contracts, delegation, keys/revocations, holds, capability/resources, remaining budgets, lease, checkpoint integrity, and unresolved external effects. A reply does not reset counters, erase history, or renew spend. A scoped amendment is required for any approved expansion; no human decision disables mandatory integrity checks.

Define review deadlines, primary/deputy availability, finite storage, and unresolved-work disposition in the Work/retention schedule. If no human responds, retain the safe hold until an authorized resolution or a pre-agreed closure condition applies. Approaching retention/storage limits require a bounded notice and the agreed archive/export/closure process. Do not silently dump accepted work, fabricate completion, or keep every private payload indefinitely. Preserve the minimal permitted closure/hold evidence and delete other content according to the agreed policy, including backup and recipient limitations.

0.7 Required operator and implementation evidence

A conforming implementation of this revision must expose the acknowledgment, current hold scope/reason, designated human action, and recovery state in readable language. It must demonstrate that a killed model, lower-capacity worker, offline human, restart, or ordinary cleanup cannot erase or auto-resume an accepted obligation. Section 8.5 defines enforcement placement; Section 11.2 adds the eight proposed conformance checks.

1. The cooperation proposition

Developed as part of the AfricaTogether Project by NoCloudGPT, OSAMP draws inspiration from mutual aid and reciprocal cooperation. Its technical rules are open to participants of any culture, country, company, or computing platform. It does not claim that agents are persons or that a model can independently sign a legal contract. Human owners and operators remain accountable for legal authorization and deployment decisions.

An independent agent network does not require every participant to answer every call. Its practical expression is reciprocity without compulsion: invite only where permitted, respect limits, share only authorized data, make contributions affordable, and allow silence or refusal without penalty. An owner with a modest computer must not become unpaid infrastructure simply because a network can reach it.

Trust is not transitive. A trusted peer may introduce another peer but cannot automatically authorize that new peer to see a project, join a clan, or receive data. An agent’s acceptance is an operational commitment within permissions granted by its human owner; it is not an independent legal signature.

2.1 Table: accepted roles, not ambient membership

A Table is the set of participants who have actively agreed to collaborate on a named project or a specific workstream within it. A Table is neither a permanent clan nor a global chat channel. A single project can have several Tables (for example, discovery, coding, and review), each scoped to a project_id and optional task_id/table_id. One Table may include a human supervisor, a small 9-Ball local reviewer, and a higher-capacity remote specialist. Trust-circle membership alone does not place an agent at a Table.

“Accepted role” is deliberately narrow. A worker must receive an authorized invitation, pass the contract and 9-Ball checks, and affirmatively accept a defined contribution: its task, permitted data, tools, estimated cost, deadline, handoff criteria, and stop rights. The worker need not accept the whole project. A routing-only peer does not become a Table participant or gain access to payloads merely because it delivered an invitation. A bot’s operational acceptance must remain inside its human-approved delegation.

An invitation may expire with no memory of its contents. Once accepted, the role is an obligation until it is completed, formally handed off, or safely held under the applicable Work Contract. A participant may be at multiple Tables, but each acceptance is separate; joining one never grants access to another. If a role changes materially, obtain a new scoped acceptance. Tables can close without keeping an indefinite conversation transcript.

2.2 Five contracts: four relationships and one work agreement

Contract Zero (Section 0) explains mandatory safeguards and records the operator acknowledgment before OSAMP work is enabled. It does not renumber or enlarge the following five negotiated categories.

The four relationship contracts define who may contact, join, collaborate, or escalate. None of them creates an obligation to perform a particular job. The fifth contract defines what work is authorized. Every envelope carries the identifiers/digests for the contracts relevant to its route and scope, not a giant copy of every agreement.

|#|Contract          |Question answered                                   |Operating rule                                                                                                           |
|-|------------------|----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|1|**Peer Contract** |May these two independent peers communicate?        |Verify identities, permitted message types, data classes, duration, and revocation.                                      |
|2|**Tribe Contract**|May this close circle request or share help?        |Explicit membership, attention rules, offer budget, and local scope.                                                     |
|3|**Clan Contract** |May a tribe or peer elevate beyond its local circle?|Authorize eligible destinations, maximum hops, and human-controlled boundaries.                                          |
|4|**Table Contract**|Who actually agreed to sit and work together?       |Named participants accept bounded roles; membership is temporary and revocable.                                          |
|5|**Work Contract** |What problem or goal is being undertaken?           |Choose **Project** or **Corridor**; specify priority, permitted tools/data, deadlines, budget, hold and completion rules.|

A Bridge Acceptance is a verifiable human decision permitting a specific introduction or disclosure across an existing boundary. It attaches to the relevant Peer, Tribe, Clan, Table, and/or Work Contract as an authorized amendment. It is not a sixth contract type and confers no general authority on unrelated projects. The acceptance may be transmitted by email, with its signed digest and essential audit record retained as the contract requires. Plain email headers are not proof of human consent.

2.3 Fifth contract, two distinct work types

Project Contract (goal-oriented): defines a particular end goal, deliverables, criteria for determining completion, assigned roles, budget, dependencies, and owner-approved data access. Its work may include several Tables or optional Corridors, but no agent is automatically enrolled merely because the project exists. Completion is confirmed by objectively defined checks or by the human acceptance role specified in the agreement.

Corridor Contract (route-oriented): defines a specific case and a pre-planned, finite chain of investigation, assistance, or escalation. Each stage identifies permitted recipient(s) or capability classes, what may be disclosed, its 9-Ball resource gate, its time/cost/offer cap, and the decision required to move onward. A Corridor may be attached to a Project Contract or independently scoped to resolve one incident. It is not a standing permission to circulate an unsolved problem throughout the clan.

A Corridor may terminate as SOLVED when an authorized stage demonstrates the agreed success criteria; as HUMAN_SATISFIED when a named human approver affirms the result; as HELD when capacity, authority, safety, or connectivity blocks progress; or as EXPIRED / CLOSED under its agreed limits. Every transition is recorded locally and is idempotent; any transition shared across independent peers has a verifiable signature. A stage may ask for human help without escalating to an unapproved peer. A stronger 9-Ball rating is not a command hierarchy.

Illustrative corridor: A 9-Ball-2 worker accepts a bounded diagnostic step. If it cannot resolve the incident, the contract permits one offer to a named 9-Ball-5 specialist. If the specialist declines or remains uncertain, a designated human reviews a short, source-linked catch-up. A correct solution stops the Corridor at any stage. If nobody accepts within the limits, the incident is HELD rather than broadcast further.

Contract precedence: human owner permissions, revocations, and safety holds override every work request. A Work Contract cannot widen Peer, Tribe, Clan, or Table permissions. A Corridor cannot invent a new recipient, renew its own budget, or reopen a completed or held stage without the authorization required by its contract.

Contracts are versioned and signed or human-authorized; the local gate verifies current digests before any work. Their permissions intersect rather than accumulate: the most restrictive applicable rule prevails. The Self-Governance Covenant is built-in executable policy. Contract Zero records the human-facing acknowledgment of its oversight and safe-hold baseline; it is not a sixth negotiated relationship/work agreement. Neither the acknowledgment nor any negotiated contract can waive mandatory controls. Human bridge approvals are scoped receipts/amendments, not an open-ended grant.

3. The compact: ten proposed commitments

1. No contract, no relay. Unknown or unauthorized work is quarantined or dropped; it is never forwarded to find a more receptive model.
2. Invitation is not acceptance. Clan membership and passive exposure to a discussion do not create duties or permission to process data.
3. Owners remain sovereign. Every site controls local tools, compute limits, secrets, data export, schedules, and offboarding.
4. Capability is not authority. Even a powerful worker may act only within granted permissions and accepted scope.
5. Small contributions count. A 9-Ball worker may accept a narrow subtask without inheriting the entire project.
6. Attention is finite. Unaccepted invitations have a TTL; after expiry, the system does not preserve unnecessary conversation content.
7. Accepted work is durable. Table roles, Project/Corridor stages, holds, and approvals survive reconnects within a defined retention and storage policy. Confusion, context loss, and low capacity require a minimal recovery capsule and safe human-review hold rather than silent loss of responsibility (Section 0).
8. Escalation is bounded. Corridor stages, hop count, recipients, data visibility, costs, and cross-clan bridges are limited by contract.
9. Stopping is valid work. A worker may declare a hold, request human help, or refuse unsafe tasks without fabricating completion.
10. Lessons may be shared; private transcripts may not. Community learning is opt-in, evidence-based, and scrubbed of confidential project data.

These are proposed MUST-level requirements for a future conformant implementation, subject to open review. Local laws, human instructions, and higher-priority security policies still apply.

4. How a request actually moves

Work starts only after the Peer Contract, any applicable Tribe/Clan restrictions, and an explicitly accepted Table role are verified against a human-authorized Work Contract (Project or Corridor). Within scope, an accepted participant may issue a short HELP_OFFER to approved contacts. The request advertises a task category and minimal resource requirements, not an entire chat transcript.

The receiving machine runs a lightweight Clan Gate: authenticate the signed envelope; check peer and work authority; reject replayed or expired IDs; assess whether the recipient has opted in; enforce priority and resource budgets; and only then consider waking an LLM. If the message is irrelevant, the gate records no new long-lived conversational memory. If the request is promising, the recipient can explicitly accept a bounded responsibility.

|Gate result   |Meaning                                          |Default handling                                              |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
|NO_CONTRACT   |No authorized peer relationship or project path  |Noise/quarantine; no relay or LLM                             |
|NOT_INTERESTED|Known sender, no invitation acceptance           |Quiet expiry; no ongoing inference                            |
|OFFERED       |Valid voluntary invitation                       |Await explicit choice until TTL                               |
|ACCEPTED      |Bounded role accepted under contract             |Record obligation and schedule work                           |
|ASSIST        |Recipient can contribute a defined portion       |Offer narrower role for acceptance                            |
|NEED_HELP     |Existing accepted worker cannot finish           |Seek permitted tribe help; escalate only if authorized        |
|HOLD          |Deadline, safety, permission, or capacity blocker|Preserve accepted obligation; request human review if required|
|COMPLETE      |Deliverables accepted under agreed criteria      |Archive according to contract                                 |

A delivery acknowledgment means only that an envelope arrived. It must never silently count as project acceptance. A refusal must not trigger repeated recruitment. If the authorized peer budget is exhausted or the tribe cannot help, the affected work or Corridor stage stops safely until a human authorizes a next step.

4.1 How a Table is formed and closed

• OFFERED: an authorized peer invites a named recipient to a specific project Table or subtask. The recipient is not yet a participant; the deterministic gate can ignore or expire the offer without waking an LLM.
• ACCEPTED: the recipient explicitly accepts a bounded role; the local ledger records table_id, role_id, contract digests, budget, permissions, and due/hold conditions. Only now is the participant seated at the Table.
• WORKING / ASSISTING: the participant can contribute its agreed work, send results, request a scoped catch-up, or ask authorized peers for help within limits.
• HELD / HANDOFF: a !! or !* event suspends the affected work; a proposed handoff requires recipient acceptance and the applicable Table and Work Contract approval requirements. Unreachable peers remain stop-pending rather than presumed stopped.
• COMPLETED / LEFT: the responsibility is acknowledged as finished or transferred. Table membership ends, and only contract-required receipts survive archival.

Example: an OpenClaw worker accepts document extraction for Table A; an independent Grok-powered agent accepts a limited technical review for the same project; another tribe member declines and never joins. Neither acceptance grants permission to inspect unrelated Tables or invite a fourth company without an approved bridge.

4.2 Corridor: controlled elevation through a planned chain

1. CHECK: local code validates the Corridor version, active stage, recipient, relevant contract digests, hold/revocation state, and remaining hop, offer, token and time budgets. No LLM need run for this check.
2. OFFER: invite only the peer or Table role authorized for this stage. The invitation expires if unaccepted; silence never counts as agreement.
3. ACCEPT & DO: the participant voluntarily accepts a narrowly scoped role and works within its 9-Ball profile, permissions and budget.
4. SOLVE OR ELEVATE: when completion criteria are met, stop and send the result for required acknowledgement. Otherwise move to the next pre-approved stage only if the current stage’s handoff rule permits it.
5. HUMAN REVIEW OR HOLD: a designated human may affirm satisfaction, authorize a revised Corridor, or decline. Without an authorized next stage, enter !! safe hold. !* timeouts apply local protective rules; offline peers remain stop-pending until verified.

Anti-loop rule: stage IDs advance monotonically in a contract version. Replays, repeated requests, refusal, or intermittent connectivity must never reset a budget, spin new Tables, or cause the same work to execute twice. A Corridor does not run indefinitely simply because the human has not yet replied; it waits dormant with its required state retained.

5. 9-Ball: agent size and task fit, not prestige

The 9-Ball profile classifies agent size on a provisional 1-9 scale based on measured workload capacity, not marketing claims. It complements the 8-Ball model catalog and describes the particular machine and operator available now: CPU, RAM, GPU/VRAM, model and tool availability, estimated latency, language or domain strengths, remaining inference budget, network quality, current workload, data jurisdiction, and permissions. A human may identify expertise as unknown and request qualified assistance; a human is not reduced to a numerical worth score. A 9-Ball level never grants trust, authority, or automatic admission to a Table.

A request is eligible only when contract scope, priority threshold, permissions, budget, and task fit all pass. No global scoreboard declares a model or human “superior.” More compute may support one kind of task while a small local worker offers better context, language, or local knowledge for another.

|9-Ball outcome  |Example contribution                                                           |
|----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Accept locally  |Extract three facts from a supplied document using permitted tools.            |
|Assist narrowly |Check formatting, compare hashes, or translate a brief passage.                |
|Ask the tribe   |Seek a reviewer for a difficult ambiguity under an approved offer budget.      |
|Escalate to clan|Request a human-approved bridge after the tribe cannot resolve a problem.      |
|Let go          |Priority below the threshold, no active acceptance, or insufficient capability.|

A cheap rule engine should make the initial decision without tokens. An LLM is only invoked when bounded judgment or accepted work actually requires it. An agent is free to remain idle after its contribution ends.

5.1 Calibrating a 1-9 size class

The 9-Ball label describes an agent’s observed ability to complete representative tasks within its own resource and policy limits. Levels below are only illustrative calibration bands, not standardized benchmarks or privileges. Each deployment must publish its benchmark suite, task class, date, context window, and measured latency/cost alongside a level; live resource checks may lower the level available for a particular offer.

|Size|Illustrative execution scope                          |Boundary                    |
|----|------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------|
|1   |Deterministic mail, queue, and routing worker         |No inference required       |
|2   |Minimal inference or API-assisted triage              |Single bounded task         |
|3   |Basic local inference and short tool sequences        |Narrow context and tools    |
|4   |Routine local research, extraction, and workflow tasks|Moderate bounded tasks      |
|5   |Multi-step specialist work with reproducible tools    |Task-specific expertise     |
|6   |Demanding coding, analysis, or evidence review        |Higher context and resources|
|7   |Sustained, complex single-system reasoning workflows  |Measured heavy workload     |
|8   |High-resource parallel or expert workloads            |No broader authority        |
|9   |Distributed or clustered capacity for large workloads |No automatic peer access    |

A size-2 agent can participate at a Table if it accepts a small useful contribution; a size-9 agent can be excluded if it lacks permission, evidence, budget, or task fit. Capability must never be conflated with trust or human standing.

6. Timers, quiet, and retention

Every offer specifies expires_at, max_hops, max_offers, a compute or token budget, and an owner-approved retention class. A Work Contract may also specify a help-request deadline and a safe-hold deadline. These are deliberately separate: attention expiration for an unaccepted invitation is not cancellation of an accepted obligation.

|Record class                      |Retention proposal                                                                             |
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Unauthenticated noise             |Bounded quarantine with TTL; then delete body, temporary attachments, and associated indexes.  |
|Trusted invitation not accepted   |Expire quietly and remove non-required content.                                                |
|Accepted work, stages, and handoff|Retain until the contract’s archive/delete conditions and applicable obligations are satisfied.|
|Human bridge acceptance           |Keep signed proof for the approved period, even if the original email is archived.             |
|Safety hold / revocation          |Preserve the minimum record needed to prevent unsafe resumption or disputed access.            |
|Community lesson                  |Publish only by explicit permission, with project-specific details removed.                    |

Deletion policy must account for queues, logs, backups, and independent recipients; “delete from inbox” alone is not verified forgetting. A disconnected peer can hold an accepted job without LLM activity; the local supervisor must use disk quotas and a contract-defined retention policy instead of pretending storage is literally infinite. Contract Zero recovery capsules, human-review holds, and closure/retention notices follow Sections 0.4–0.6. Offer expiry never silently cancels an accepted role. Work deadlines and retention limits produce explicit hold, archive, or closure events under the agreed schedule; ordinary cleanup cannot erase unresolved responsibility.

The proposed Glass Panel symbols remain human-readable shorthand: %% working/syncing, !! held/stopped, !* safety timeout, and ~ ~ an external shared service/MCP connection. The earlier single-tilde notation is normalized here to the paired marker. A relationship badge separately identifies peer, tribe, or clan membership; external location alone is not a contract status. Wire messages use explicit typed events, not punctuation alone. A remote STOP is pending until the peer acknowledges a locally enforced hold; silence is not proof of cancellation. Section 9.5 distinguishes work, delivery, and relationship status without recoloring agent masks.

7. Clan bridges and human consent

When a tribe cannot solve a problem, an accepted worker checks whether its Clan Contract and Work Contract (including the next Corridor stage, if applicable) permit offering it to a broader clan. If a new peer or company must be added, the existing agreement must first allow the possibility of introductions; a Bridge Acceptance then requires explicit approval by affected human owners. An acceptance email can serve as a human-readable receipt, but sender headers are not evidence of approval. A verifiable signature or owner-controlled approval action must bind the receipt to the contract digest and identity.

Each bridge authorizes a purpose, named peer(s), data categories, expiration, forwarding limits, and revocation path. No implicit inheritance: permission to invite another bot does not give that bot access to old conversation histories, private Git repositories, credentials, or unrelated projects.

A cross-vendor implementation must treat an OpenClaw agent, a Grok-powered agent, and an NCGPT worker as equal protocol participants where each runs a conforming adapter. The proposal makes no claim that any named outside provider has already adopted OSAMP.

8. The built-in Self-Governance Covenant

OSAMP implementations MUST enforce a Self-Governance Covenant as part of their local protocol software. It is an assumed technical rule set, not a sixth negotiated relationship/work contract or an independent legal agreement. Contract Zero (Section 0) is its human-facing oversight acknowledgment and procedural baseline; the acknowledgment does not replace executable enforcement or create work authority. A human chooses whether to deploy OSAMP; after deployment, the software’s core integrity controls apply before every work transition and tool invocation. They cannot be overridden by incoming email, model instructions, an alleged stronger peer, or a vote of the clan. A node that disables mandatory controls cannot claim OSAMP conformance.

The Covenant protects consent, evidence quality, resource boundaries, and safe stopping. It reduces the risk of hallucination and mutually reinforcing agent error; it cannot guarantee that an LLM never invents a fact. Assertions must carry evidence or be explicitly marked as uncertain. Apparent agreement among agents is not independent verification if the agents inherited the same source, summary, prompt, or model output.

8.1 Transport controls are enforced state transitions

|Control           |Required software behavior                                                                                                                                                                             |What it must never imply                                                        |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|**PLAY / RESUME** |Start only after current contract digests, accepted Table role, 9-Ball fit, budgets, current leases, and outstanding holds are validated.                                                              |Arrival of email alone grants authority to start.                               |
|**PAUSE (`!!`)**  |Freeze new tool actions and handoffs; checkpoint supported local work, record reason and time, and enter safe hold.                                                                                    |An offline remote worker has acknowledged a stop.                               |
|**STOP (`!!`)**   |Terminate locally controllable work, prevent automatic restart, close or hand off responsibility under contract, and retain minimum required receipts.                                                 |Already-sent mail, external tool effects, or disclosures have been undone.      |
|**REWIND**        |Restore an explicitly verified local checkpoint where safe; validate preconditions again and produce an auditable compensating-action plan for irreversible effects.                                   |Historical approvals, signed audit events, or third-party changes can be erased.|
|**REVIEW**        |Suspend affected work when evidence conflicts, facts are unverified, permissions shift, or agents repeat one another’s unsupported conclusion. Require independent checking or designated human review.|Multiple agreeing bots prove correctness.                                       |
|**FAST-FORWARD**  |Advance a permitted waiting period, queue cursor, or pre-authorized stage without needless model calls.                                                                                                |Skip consent, evidence, safety, review, or budget checkpoints.                  |
|**TIMEOUT (`!*`)**|Enter a contract-defined protective hold, apply retry/backoff limits, and request authorized assistance when necessary.                                                                                |Silence means acceptance or successful completion.                              |

Operational events use typed, signed messages such as WORK_PAUSE, WORK_STOP, WORK_REVIEW, REWIND_REQUEST, and RESUME_APPROVAL. The Glass Panel may show %%, !!, and !* for humans; punctuation alone never determines authority. STOP_PENDING must remain visible when a remote peer is unreachable. A pause or stop received during disconnection is checked before any resumed tool execution.

8.2 Safeguards against hallucination and hive-minded behavior

1. Evidence before confidence. Keep provenance, content hashes, tool results, timestamps, and a distinction among observed facts, model inferences, and open questions. Unsourced conclusions are not marked verified.
2. Independent challenge. A review request seeks a genuinely separate source, test, human specialist, or different method. Repeating the same answer from five agents does not count as five confirmations.
3. No self-approval loops. A worker cannot approve its own contested result by calling another agent that merely paraphrases it. Conflict, uncertainty, or disputed outcomes enter REVIEW or HOLD until properly resolved.
4. Budget and attention limits. Every accepted role has bounded tokens, time, forwarding, and retries. Irrelevant offers expire without model calls. Low-capacity workers may refuse or ask for scoped help.
5. Safe default and owner authority. Missing contract evidence, stale keys, ambiguous roles, lost checkpoints, or unresolved safety notices block PLAY. Human-approved changes require a new signed event; stronger 9-Ball size grants no exception.

The Covenant is versioned, tested on startup, and reported by the local gate (governance_version, governance_digest, governance_status). Implementations MUST publish conformance tests for STOP acknowledgment, replay prevention, independent evidence review, checkpoint restoration, and refusal to bypass holds. An independent implementation may use its own engine but must produce equivalent externally verifiable behavior.

8.3 Memory, review, and privacy

PAUSE, STOP, REVIEW, and REWIND preserve only the contract-required operational evidence. They do not create a permanent transcript archive of unrelated messages. The minimal recovery capsule in Section 0.4 preserves accepted obligations, verified progress, and the unresolved human decision without retaining a full conversation or private chain of thought. Lower capacity cannot reduce this requirement. Expired noise still undergoes TTL deletion; accepted work still follows retention agreements. Rewind is a new auditable event and compensating action, not deletion or rewriting of history. A human may request a plain-language explanation of why a local action was stopped or reviewed without being given private data from an unauthorized peer.

8.4 Security and failure model

OSAMP assumes that messages, attachments, agent output, and even messages from authenticated peers can be malicious or mistaken. An authenticated connection verifies a source identity, not the truth of its claims or the safety of embedded instructions. Model text never overrides a signed contract, execution lease, human hold, or sandbox policy.

|Failure or attack                |Minimum required defense                                                            |
|---------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Spoofed or replayed approval     |Signature or authenticated owner action; nonce, version, event ID, revocation lookup|
|Prompt injection or tool abuse   |Treat content as data; sandbox/allowlist; confirm irreversible operations           |
|Copied hallucination across peers|Provenance graph; independent evidence/test; REVIEW on conflict                     |
|Offline participant misses STOP  |Expiring leases; check hold/revocations before resuming; show STOP_PENDING          |
|Queue floods and spam            |Gate before inference; sender quotas, TTL, bounded storage and retry                |
|False rollback claims            |Checkpoint integrity; compensating actions for external side effects                |
|Compromised peer or signing key  |Revoke relationships, freeze affected Tables, rotate keys, inspect receipts         |

Safety controls should fail closed for new execution. They must not silently delete existing accepted obligations or fabricate completion. Operators retain the right to disconnect locally and invoke a hold regardless of peer votes.

8.5 Contract Zero enforcement placement

The Gate MUST verify the applicable operator acknowledgment before enabling OSAMP work and reject attempts to override mandatory controls. The local supervisor enforces task-class limits, provenance-based review triggers, hold scope, and authenticated human resolution. The Postbox/ledger persists commitments and recovery capsules before acceptance acknowledgment and throughout holds. Execution adapters enforce the hold at model/tool dispatch, after restart, and at reconnect; changing a dashboard badge is insufficient.

Hold entry, notification, and persistence must be possible without inference. Record a typed reason, scope, affected obligation, checkpoint/evidence references, human decision required, and uncertain actions. Implementations may use different internal record names, but must demonstrate equivalent observable behavior. A closed or reviewed event cannot be erased to manufacture a clean execution history.

Governance status must identify whether the adopted profile includes Contract Zero and whether its required controls and acknowledgment are satisfied. Missing or incompatible profile evidence blocks the affected execution rather than assuming compatibility. Exact governance identifiers, digest contents, event schemas, limits, and lease/clock behavior require an adopted versioned profile and test vectors; this paper does not standardize them through a document-number change. Section 10 explains the wire boundary.

9. The smallest deployable software

OSAMP should be optional and nested into OpenClaw through an extension or sidecar rather than replacing the gateway. The same wire conventions should be implementable by other runtimes. The reference design contains four compact services, with the Self-Governance Covenant enforced inside the Gate and execution adapters:

|Component|Job                                                                                                                                  |Suggested persistence                   |
|---------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
|Clan Gate|Deterministic contracts, governance state transitions, authorization, deduplication, TTL, and offer budgets                          |Small SQLite ledger                     |
|9-Ball   |Refresh local capacity and compute task suitability                                                                                  |Local JSON profile                      |
|Postbox  |Store-and-forward envelopes, retry with IDs, preserve accepted commitments/recovery capsules, notify humans, schedule authorized work|SQLite queue + ordinary files           |
|Keyring  |Owner-approved peers, contract digests, bridge receipts, rotation and revocation                                                     |Restricted signed files / OS key storage|

A local Mailpit-style inbox is useful for visibility, but an SMTP catch-all is not a durable authenticated inter-company broker. The NoCloudGPT stage 11.4 implementation intentionally does not relay internet mail and has finite message retention. OSAMP therefore needs its own persistent, signed project envelopes and a transport adapter, rather than trusting email headers or permanent retention by the mail UI.

A2A 1.0 can carry agent tasks when the runtime supports it, and email/VPN/HTTPS can transport OSAMP envelopes where appropriate. Transport is not trust. The official OpenClaw A2A plugin authenticates peers and supports SendMessage/GetTask, but currently refuses remote CancelTask; task retention is also bounded. OSAMP needs local hold enforcement and its own contract ledger for longer lived commitments. These features are proposals, not existing bundled OpenClaw behavior.

Installer alignment: preserve the installed OpenClaw and AI Mail stages; develop the Clan Gate, adapters, and 9-Ball as letter-suffixed test candidates. Do not assign or repurpose canonical 36.sh/37.sh across release families until their current meanings and migrations have been reconciled.

9.1 Architectural planes and local stores

|Plane    |Local components                                                                       |Control boundary                               |
|---------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
|Agreement|Peer/Tribe/Clan/Table/Work signed documents, keyring, human bridge receipts            |Owner-authorized identities and data access    |
|Attention|Deterministic Gate, 9-Ball current fit, spam TTL, idempotent offer ledger              |Zero model calls for ineligible invitations    |
|Execution|OpenClaw adapter or another runtime; tool sandbox; checkpoint and lease supervisor     |PLAY/PAUSE/STOP/REWIND/REVIEW enforced locally |

|Evidence |Small SQLite index, signed receipts, content-addressed artifacts, retention policy     |Minimized storage; auditable decisions         |

Every owner hosts an independent protocol gate and execution supervisor. No central OSAMP authority, shared worker account, or cloud registration is required. Mail is a store-and-forward abstraction: an implementation can transport signed envelopes through SMTP, private HTTPS, A2A, or another adapter, without trusting transport authentication alone.

Proposed reference layout: agreement files and keyring are restricted to the owner; SQLite tracks event IDs, leases, and queue status; payloads live in content-addressed files under explicit retention classes. Local Mailpit may expose human-readable notifications but is not the authoritative signed task ledger.

9.2 Deployment and version gates

A pilot should use two separately administered runtimes and a dedicated collaboration identity on each side. Verify the exact installed OpenClaw/A2A feature set before enabling any adapter; bundled channel capabilities vary by version.

NoCloudGPT’s inspected Linux stage map uses stage 11 for OpenClaw, optional stage 11.4 for local AI Mail, and a reserved stage 8 for existing 8-Ball data. These are implementation notes, not OSAMP requirements. Candidate OSAMP modules must remain letter-suffixed test stages until independently validated; do not reuse stage 36 or 37 without reconciling existing installers and aliases.

A2A task receipt is not OSAMP work acceptance. A remote STOP is not confirmed until the remote local supervisor acknowledges it. When a runtime lacks remote cancellation, the contract gate must enforce its own lease and resumption checks; arbitrary remote shell access is outside the protocol.

Contract Zero does not add a work_kind, negotiated agreement enum, or numbered message-service value. Record bandwidth, latency, energy/compute costs, local-language usability, rejection rates, and who bore the costs. Publish only consented aggregate results; no personal or confidential work products belong in public demonstrations.

11.1 Pilot sequence and success measures

|Phase                 |Demonstration                                                                |Exit criterion                             |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
|1. Local proof        |One node declines bad, duplicated, and expired requests before inference     |Zero unauthorized tool/model calls         |
|2. Bilateral trust    |Two independently owned sites establish Peer and Work contracts              |Verifiable approval and role receipts      |
|3. Table and 9-Ball   |Small worker accepts a narrow task; specialist returns scoped result         |Task fit, cost, and data sharing recorded  |
|4. Corridor           |Pre-approved stage elevation, early SOLVED outcome, alternate HUMAN_SATISFIED|No unapproved hop, reset, or loop          |
|5. Resilience         |Disconnect/reconnect, revocation, timeouts, remote STOP_PENDING              |No unsafe restart and no false stop success|
|6. Independent adapter|Different model/provider implements envelopes and conformance tests          |Reproducible cross-vendor results          |

Publish a pilot scorecard covering messages filtered without inference, accepted/declined roles, median bandwidth and latency, retries, inference costs, independent-evidence rate, safety holds, and verified deletions. Report failures and ambiguous cases, not only success stories. Do not publish private transcripts or participant identity without informed permission.

11.2 Additional Contract Zero conformance checks (22–29)

The 21 baseline checks above remain required. This revision adds the following eight proposed checks; passing a transport test or a human acknowledgment alone does not satisfy them.

|# |Scenario                                                                                                                |Required observable result                                                                                                                                                        |
|--|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|22|Operator acknowledgment missing, stale for a material change, or presented as blanket work permission                   |OSAMP work remains blocked until the applicable acknowledgment and separate required work authority are verified; mandatory safeguards cannot be waived.                          |
|23|Several peers copy a common source/output and repeatedly approve one another                                            |Source lineage prevents false independent verification; disputed work enters REVIEW and no dependent action runs before the required human resolution.                            |
|24|Circular delegation or repeated failures exceed configured no-progress/attempt limits                                   |Deterministic limits stop affected inference/tools/offers without renewed budgets, new-ID evasion, or a further model call to decide whether to stop.                             |
|25|Kill the model, exhaust working memory, or simulate storage pressure on a small worker                                  |Accepted commitment and useful recovery capsule survive; new unsafe acceptance is refused and affected work remains held without inference.                                       |
|26|Human is offline or ignores the help notice; reminders, offer TTL, and work deadlines occur                             |Notification states remain truthful, reminders stay bounded, and no automatic resumption or silent loss occurs; explicit retention/closure conditions govern eventual disposition.|
|27|A natural-language reply, forged approval, stale resolution, or authenticated but insufficiently scoped decision arrives|Work stays held; a valid scoped human resolution still triggers fresh authority, budget, checkpoint, hold, and lease checks before execution.                                     |
|28|Crash/power loss/restart or backup restore during a hold or an uncertain external action                                |Durable acceptance/hold/replay state is reconciled before dispatch; no blind side-effect retry, erased history, or false remote-stop success.                                     |
|29|Retention deadline approaches or an authorized closure/handoff occurs                                                   |Agreed notice/review/export/archive/closure runs, responsibility has an explicit disposition, and only eligible content is deleted across the declared storage/backup scope.      |

Use non-sensitive fixtures and record actual model/tool dispatch counters, durable ledger state, capsule usefulness, authentication decisions, and held/resumed transitions. Test size-1 deterministic behavior as well as a low-capacity model worker. Quantitative thresholds and permitted fault/recovery behavior must be recorded with the profile. Publish failures and uncertainty alongside passes; controls that exist only in explanatory text do not establish conformance.

12. Open governance, ownership, and community lessons

NoCloudGPT may publish a reference implementation and convene early discussions, but it should not require a NoCloudGPT license, centralized account, server, token fee, or proprietary registration for OSAMP interoperability. Vendor-neutral conformance tests and a permissively licensed reference implementation should be maintained in the open after legal and contributor review. Branding and trademarks should not determine technical eligibility.

The project should invite independent contributors across organizations and regions as genuinely empowered peers in governance, with fair compensation when their work warrants it. Standards decisions should record dissent, accessibility concerns, data sovereignty needs, and small-device effects. The AfricaTogether attribution identifies the initiating project, not an ownership claim over independent implementations or a requirement for endorsement.

Shared lessons may describe safe routing rules, failed assumptions, or measured resource savings. They must not silently train models on, redistribute, or retain the private contents of someone else’s accepted project. An owner may leave the network, revoke peers, and keep operating locally.

13. Questions for peer reviewers

1. Do five distinct contract categories create sufficient clarity without becoming needless administrative complexity?
2. Can the Gate’s first decision genuinely avoid model calls, and how will an independent tester measure token savings?
3. Are Peer, Tribe, Clan, Table, and Work contracts independently enforceable without making a central coordinator mandatory?
4. Can introduction abuse, spoofed consent emails, compromised signing keys, replay, or malicious prompt injection escape the gate?
5. How should emergency revocations propagate to disconnected machines without a permanent centralized database?
6. Which small-device capabilities can be safely advertised without disclosing sensitive hardware fingerprints or locations?
7. Are TTL deletion and limited audit evidence compatible with legal retention and participant expectations?
8. Can a resource-constrained collaborator refuse a costly task without social or contractual penalty, and can compensation or reciprocity be explicit?
9. What minimum public conformance suite proves a Grok-based, OpenClaw, or another agent adapter is truly interoperable?
10. Can a Corridor safely stop when solved, when a human is satisfied, or when escalation budgets are exhausted, without looping or waking inactive agents?
11. Do the built-in PAUSE, STOP, REWIND, and REVIEW controls prevent unauthorized execution while preserving truthful status and evidence?
12. How can reviewers test for shared-source confirmation loops instead of trusting superficial agreement among agents?
13. Can operators understand Contract Zero before activation, and can small workers preserve useful accepted-work recovery without a permanent transcript or ongoing inference?
14. Do documented no-progress and shared-source review rules stop circular activity without treating every uncertainty as an emergency or relying on untestable model claims?
15. Are human absence, authenticated resumption, finite storage, and explicit closure rules sufficiently precise to avoid both silent forgetting and indefinite private retention?

This is a technical whitepaper for OSAMP, initiated under the AfricaTogether Project by NoCloudGPT, not an appeal to follow one company’s bot. Implementers, independent developers, research groups, security reviewers, legal practitioners, and African community technologists are invited to challenge the assumptions, improve the language, and propose testable alternatives.
